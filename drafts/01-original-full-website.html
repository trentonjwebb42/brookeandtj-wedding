Two thousand twenty-seven

A long weekend on the Adriatic, and you, we hope, beside us.

Polignano a Mare sits on a shelf of pale limestone above the sea, its houses built right to the edge of the cliff. In early September the water is still warm and the evenings run long. We'd love you to spend a few days there with us: a welcome supper, a wedding among the olive trees at Villa Torre Bianca, and a last slow day on the water before everyone scatters home.

This site will grow as plans settle. Travel notes, places to stay and our favourite tables in town are all here, with more to come.

III.Travel & Stay

Getting there and where to rest your head

By air BRIBari

Fly into Bari Karol Wojtyła Airport, the closest international airport to Polignano a Mare. Most routes from the US connect through a major European hub.

By car ~45minutes

Polignano is a short drive south-east along the coast from the airport. Taxis and private transfers wait at arrivals; car hire is easy if you plan to explore.

Approximate · to be confirmed
By train FSregional

Regional trains run from Bari Centrale to Polignano a Mare station, a short walk from the old town. We'll post the simplest airport-to-town route here.

Before you go
Book early
September is still high season on this coast. Reserve flights and rooms as soon as you can.
Passports
Check your passport's expiry date well ahead of travel. We'll post current entry requirements closer to the date.
Arrive a day early
Give yourself time for jet lag before Thursday's supper, especially if you're flying from the US.
Shoes for stone
The old town is all steps and smooth limestone. Bring flat shoes for the day and something sturdy for the evening.
